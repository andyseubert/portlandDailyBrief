Sahuarita Unified Governing Board — What to Watch

Wednesday, August 12, 2026

Governing Board Meeting — District Auditorium, Building #21, Sub-Hall B, 350 W. Sahuarita Road, Sahuarita, AZ 85641

SUSD Board to Consider Major Policy Overhaul on Student Admissions and Superintendent Qualifications at Aug. 12 Meeting

The board will consider a sweeping first reading of 16 policy advisories — including several new regulations governing how the district admits resident, nonresident, transfer, homeless, and foster-care students — alongside a Prop 301 performance pay plan that directly affects teacher compensation. The meeting also includes a financial compliance resolution and a large personnel roster reflecting back-to-school hiring across the district.

1. Sweeping Student Admissions Policy Rewrite Goes Before the Board

What it is: The board will consider a first reading of seven new or revised regulations governing student admissions — covering resident students, nonresident tuition enrollment, transfer students, homeless students, students in foster care, and open enrollment (Policy Advisories 1055–1061). Because these are a first reading, no vote will be taken tonight, but the proposals open a formal public review window before adoption.

Why it matters: Admissions and open-enrollment rules determine which students can access SUSD schools and under what conditions — directly intersecting with the district's ongoing enrollment pressure from the statewide ESA voucher program. New or tightened rules on nonresident and transfer admissions could affect hundreds of families along the I-19 corridor who cross attendance boundaries, while updated homeless and foster-care regulations carry federal McKinney-Vento compliance implications.

This is a New Business – Governing Board item (first reading; no board action tonight).

2. Superintendent Qualifications Policy Up for First Review

What it is: Policy Advisory 1048 proposes revisions to Policy CBA, which defines the qualifications and duties of the superintendent — the district's top leadership position. Like the admissions package, this is a first reading.

Why it matters: Any change to the superintendent's defined qualifications or duties touches the most consequential hiring and accountability decision a governing board makes. With district leadership directly shaping academic outcomes, budget priorities, and staff retention for roughly 6,400 students, how the board defines and constrains that role is of significant public interest — particularly if the revision expands or narrows experience requirements or alters the board's oversight relationship with the superintendent.

This is a New Business – Governing Board item (first reading; no board action tonight).

3. Board to Consider Changes to Bidding and Purchasing Procedures

What it is: Policy Advisory 1050 proposes revisions to Policy DJE, which governs how the district solicits bids and makes purchases — the rules that govern every contract, vendor selection, and large expenditure.

Why it matters: Purchasing policy sets the guardrails on how taxpayer dollars are spent and which vendors compete for district contracts. Changes to bidding thresholds or sole-source exemptions can have significant fiscal consequences for a district that regularly undertakes facilities maintenance, technology upgrades, and transportation contracts. Any loosening or tightening of competitive-bid requirements is worth close scrutiny.

This is a New Business – Governing Board item (first reading; no board action tonight).

4. Prop 301 Performance Pay Plan on the Table

What it is: The board will consider the district's Proposition 301 Performance Pay Plan, which governs how a portion of state sales-tax revenue earmarked for education is distributed to teachers and staff as performance-based compensation.

Why it matters: Prop 301 funds are a meaningful component of take-home pay for SUSD educators, and the structure of the performance pay plan — who qualifies, how much they receive, and what metrics are used — affects teacher retention at a time when the district is actively backfilling vacancies (as the lengthy new-hire list on tonight's consent agenda illustrates). The board's approval or modification of this plan sets compensation expectations for the coming school year.

This is a New Business – Personnel item.

5. Financial Compliance Resolution on Staff Meals

What it is: The board will consider a resolution related to the Uniform System of Financial Records (USFR) update regarding staff meals — a state-mandated accounting framework that governs how Arizona school districts handle and document certain expenditures.

Why it matters: USFR compliance resolutions are required when district practice must align with updated Arizona Auditor General guidance. A resolution specific to staff meals signals the district is formalizing or correcting how it documents meal-related expenditures — an area that has drawn audit findings in Arizona districts before. Passing the resolution keeps the district in compliance and reduces audit risk.

This is a New Business – Business item.

6. Large Back-to-School Hiring Wave and Staff Departures Reflect Ongoing Workforce Churn

What it is: The consent agenda includes more than 80 employment actions — new hires, status changes, and pay adjustments — alongside 16 resignations and retirements. New hires span classroom teachers at multiple campuses, special education inclusion assistants, bus drivers, a speech-language pathologist, a school counselor, and a new procurement specialist in the business office. Departures include teachers, a transition specialist in student services, a maintenance technician, and a bus driver.

Why it matters: The volume and breadth of hiring just before the school year begins underscores persistent staffing pressure across instruction, special education, and transportation — the same categories that have driven district-wide concern about service delivery. Notably, one addendum flags a "hard to fill position" supplement at Sahuarita High School, an explicit signal that the district is using financial incentives to recruit in shortage areas. The concurrent wave of resignations — several dated as far back as May — suggests some positions may have gone weeks without coverage heading into the new school year.

These are Consent Agenda items.
